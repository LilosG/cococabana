Oceanside's food scene has quietly grown into something worth the drive, especially if you're hunting for Caribbean small plates that actually taste like they came from someone who knows the islands. The pier-side location and beach-town vibe make it a natural fit for island-inspired cuisine, but not every spot nails it. We've tracked down where to find the real deal—starting with the rooftop that's changed the game.

## Cococabana: Jerk Butter Oysters and Rooftop Views

Cococabana sits on top of The Brick Hotel at 408 Pier View Way, and it's the only place in downtown Oceanside where you're eating Caribbean small plates while watching the Pacific unfold below you. The rooftop location alone sets it apart, but the food is what keeps people coming back.

The Jerk Butter Charbroiled Oysters are the signature move. Fresh oysters get charred over a live flame, then finished with a compound butter spiked with jerk spices—scotch bonnet heat, thyme, allspice—that doesn't overpower the brine. They arrive still smoking, and the butter pools in the shell. Order them. The Wagyu Skewers come next: tender beef threaded with charred pineapple, a glaze that leans sweet and savory without tipping into dessert territory. The skewers work as a standalone plate or as part of a larger spread.

The bar program leans into rum and Caribbean spirits, which means your cocktail menu isn't generic. The drinks pair naturally with the small plates—citrus-forward, spirit-forward, nothing overly complicated. Service moves at the pace of a rooftop bar that respects both efficiency and the fact that you're here to linger. Cococabana operates at the higher end of Oceanside pricing, but the rooftop real estate and quality of execution justify it. Reserve ahead, especially for sunset.

If you're planning a night out, check out Cococabana's spot as the [best rooftop bar oceanside](/blog/best-rooftop-bar-oceanside/) for the full experience.

## Duke's La Jolla: Seafood-Focused Island Bites

Duke's sits a short drive north in La Jolla, but it's worth the trip if you want Caribbean-inspired small plates with a seafood-first philosophy. The space overlooks the water, the vibe is decidedly beachy, and the kitchen leans into fresh catch and tropical flavors.

Their ceviche preparations rotate with the market, but when they're running a citrus-cured fish with avocado and crispy plantain chips, it's solid. The fish is impeccably fresh—you can taste the difference. Grilled fish tacos with a mango-habanero salsa show restraint and flavor balance. Nothing feels heavy or overseasoned. The poke bowl, if they're running it, comes with soy-ginger and sesame, more Pacific Rim than strictly Caribbean, but it works on a small plates menu.

The cocktails skew tropical without becoming cartoonish. The service is friendly but can lag during peak hours. Parking is street-only and competitive, especially weekends. Duke's occupies a middle ground—more casual than Cococabana, less focused on the Caribbean angle, but reliable for fresh seafood and island-adjacent flavors. Prices are moderate to moderate-high.

## Wrench & Rodent Seabasstropub: Experimental Island Approach

Wrench & Rodent operates as a seafood-focused spot with a playful name and an experimental kitchen. Located in Oceanside, they take Caribbean and global influences and remix them constantly. Small plates here aren't a set menu—they're a reflection of what the kitchen is excited about that week.

You might find charred fish collars with a citrus-fermented hot sauce, or grilled octopus with chimichurri and cassava. The kitchen respects ingredient quality and isn't afraid of bold flavors. The vibe is intentionally casual and a little irreverent—this isn't fine dining, it's smart cooking in a relaxed setting.

The cocktails lean into rum and Caribbean spirits, though the bar program isn't as developed as Cococabana's. Prices are moderate. The downside: consistency can vary, and during slower service periods, the kitchen moves slowly. The upside: if you want to explore Caribbean flavors through a creative lens, you'll find something unexpected here. It's worth a visit if you're willing to embrace the experimental nature.

## Oceanside Pier Fish Market: Casual Counter Bites

The Oceanside Pier Fish Market sits literally on the pier, and it's where locals grab fresh fish, prepared simply. The small plates here aren't haute Caribbean—they're straightforward, honest, and cheap. Grilled fish tacos, ceviche with avocado, shrimp ceviche, grilled mahi-mahi plates. Everything is built on fresh catch, minimal fuss.

If you want Caribbean small plates without the rooftop price tag or the craft cocktail program, this is the spot. You order at the counter, grab a seat at a picnic table or take it to go, and eat overlooking the water. The fish quality is the main event. Prices are low. The experience is decidedly casual—no reservations, no ambiance beyond the pier and the ocean. It's ideal for lunch or an afternoon snack, less ideal for a planned dinner.

## Lolita's Mexican Food: Unexpected Island Fusion

Lolita's is primarily a Mexican restaurant, but their kitchen plays with Caribbean and Latin fusion in ways that work. The ceviche here leans Mexican-style (more lime, cilantro, jalapeño), but they run occasional preparations with tropical fruit and island spices that bridge both traditions. The grilled fish tacos are excellent—fresh fish, simple prep, good tortillas.

It's not a Caribbean-focused menu, but if you're looking for small plates that incorporate island flavors within a broader Latin framework, Lolita's delivers. Prices are low to moderate. The vibe is casual and family-friendly. Service is warm and efficient. It's a solid option if you want variety without leaving downtown Oceanside.

## How to Choose: Rooftop Experience vs. Casual Bites

Your choice depends on what you're after. If you want the full experience—rooftop views, craft cocktails, polished execution, and the best Caribbean small plates in Oceanside—Cococabana is the only option. You're paying for location, service, and consistency. Reserve ahead and go at sunset.

If you want quality seafood-forward small plates with tropical flavors at a lower price point, Duke's in La Jolla or Wrench & Rodent in Oceanside are solid. Both prioritize ingredient quality and flavor balance.

If you want the cheapest route and don't mind casual, the Oceanside Pier Fish Market is unbeatable. Fresh fish, minimal markup, water views.

For something in between—casual but intentional—Lolita's or Wrench & Rodent offer good value and interesting flavor combinations.

## Logistics: Hours, Reservations, and Parking

Cococabana operates Tuesday through Sunday, 4 p.m. to close. Reservations are strongly recommended, especially for parties of four or more and during peak season (May through September). The venue is at 408 Pier View Way, downtown Oceanside. Parking is available in The Brick Hotel lot and nearby street parking. Expect rooftop pricing: small plates run $14–$22 each, cocktails $14–$18.

Duke's La Jolla is open daily, 11 a.m. to close. No reservations; first-come, first-served. Street parking only; arrive early or plan for a walk. Small plates $12–$18, cocktails $12–$16.

Wrench & Rodent is open Wednesday through Sunday, 5 p.m. to close. No reservations. Street parking. Small plates $10–$16, cocktails $12–$15.

The Oceanside Pier Fish Market is open daily, 10 a.m. to sunset (roughly). Counter service only. Pier parking is free. Small plates $8–$14, no alcohol.

Lolita's is open daily, 11 a.m. to close. No reservations. Street parking. Small plates $6–$12, beer and wine only.

## What to Order: The Must-Try Plates

At Cococabana, order the Jerk Butter Charbroiled Oysters and the Wagyu Skewers. Add the conch fritters if they're running them. Pair with a rum-forward cocktail.

At Duke's, get the ceviche and the grilled fish tacos. Order whatever fresh catch is available as a main or share plate.

At Wrench & Rodent, ask the server what the kitchen is excited about that week. Go with their recommendation.

At the Pier Fish Market, get the ceviche and a grilled fish plate. Simple, fresh, reliable.

At Lolita's, order the ceviche and the fish tacos. They're consistent and well-executed.
