A rooftop date night in Oceanside doesn't have to drain your wallet, especially when you know what to expect before you arrive. Cococabana sits 408 Pier View Way with views of the Pacific and the Oceanside Pier stretching into the distance—the kind of backdrop that makes a Tuesday feel like a celebration. But the real question most couples ask: *How much is this actually going to cost?*

Here's the honest breakdown. We've walked through what a realistic date night actually runs, factored in the cocktails, the shareable plates, and the moments that make it worth the splurge. No guesswork, no hidden fees. Just real numbers and practical planning so you can decide if it fits your evening.

## What You'll Actually Spend: The Per-Person Average

A comfortable date night at Cococabana runs **$60–$100 per person** before tax and tip. That covers two cocktails and three to four small plates shared between you. If you're both ordering entrées or going heavier on the spirits, you're looking at the higher end. If you're strategic during happy hour, you can drop that to $40–$60 per person.

Here's why that range exists: Cocktails at Cococabana run **$12–$16** each. That's standard for a rooftop venue in a beachside location where you're paying for the view, the craft, and the bartender who actually knows their way around a shaker. Small plates land between **$8–$18** depending on what you order. A shared cheese board might be $14; charred octopus or ceviche runs closer to $16–$18. Appetizers sit in the **$9–$13** range.

The math: Two cocktails ($28–$32) plus three small plates ($42–$54) puts you at **$70–$86 before tax**. Add 8% sales tax in Oceanside and you're at $76–$93. Tip on that (18–20%) brings you to roughly **$90–$112 per person** for a full evening with drinks and food.

## Breaking Down the Cocktail Cost

Cococabana's cocktail menu leans into Caribbean and tropical flavors—rum-forward drinks, fresh citrus, and the kind of names that make you feel like you're somewhere other than downtown Oceanside (even though the pier is right there). Most house cocktails run **$13–$15**. Premium spirit upgrades or signature drinks might push to **$16–$17**.

If you're both ordering two cocktails, that's **$52–$68 for the evening** on drinks alone. The bartenders here actually make their drinks to order, which means you're not paying for speed—you're paying for quality. A mojito takes two minutes because they're muddling fresh mint. That's not overhead; that's craft.

If you want to trim this line item, ask about happy hour. When Cococabana runs specials (typically earlier in the week and early evening), cocktails drop to **$10–$12**. You lose nothing on quality; you just catch the timing right.

## Small Plates: The Sharing Strategy

This is where couples save money without sacrificing the experience. Cococabana's menu is built for sharing. Three to four small plates between two people gives you variety, keeps the bill reasonable, and lets you both try more.

A realistic shared spread:
- One seafood plate (ceviche, shrimp, or octopus): **$16–$18**
- One vegetable or grain-based plate (plantains, yuca fries, or a salad): **$9–$12**
- One cheese or charcuterie board: **$14–$16**
- One hot appetizer (empanadas, fritters, or wings): **$10–$13**

Total: **$49–$59** for four plates. That's roughly **$24–$30 per person** on food.

If you're less hungry or want to keep costs down, order three plates instead of four. You'll still feel satisfied and you'll drop the food cost to **$35–$45 total**.

## The Entrée Question: When to Order Them

Here's the honest part: You don't need entrées for a date night at Cococabana. The small plates are generous enough and designed to be the star. If you order an entrée, you're adding **$18–$28 per plate** to your bill, which pushes your per-person total to **$120–$150+**. That's a different experience—still great, but a different budget.

Entrées make sense if:
- You're making it a longer evening and want more substance
- You're celebrating something specific (anniversary, promotion, etc.)
- You're not splitting plates and want your own main course

For a standard date night, skip the entrées and lean into the small plates. You'll eat better, spend less, and the meal will feel more like an adventure than a traditional dinner.

## Factoring in Dessert and After-Dinner Drinks

Most couples don't budget for dessert at a rooftop bar, but Cococabana offers it. Desserts run **$7–$10** and are worth considering if you're staying for another round of drinks. An after-dinner cocktail (a digestif, coffee drink, or something lighter) runs the same **$12–$16** as any other cocktail.

If you add dessert and one after-dinner drink each, that's an extra **$40–$50 per person**. It pushes your total closer to **$130–$160 per person**, but it also extends your evening by an hour and changes the whole vibe. Sometimes that's the point.

## Happy Hour and Off-Peak Pricing

Cococabana's happy hour is where the math shifts in your favor. During these windows, cocktails drop to **$10–$12** and select appetizers get **$2–$3 discounts**. If you time your date night for happy hour (usually weekday early evenings), you can cut your per-person cost to **$45–$65**.

That's not a compromise on quality—it's just smart timing. The rooftop views don't change. The bartenders are the same. The food is the same. You're just paying less because fewer people are there.
